Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "Deadline-Aware Adaptive-Rank Federated LoRA over Mobile Edge Networks" for publication in Computer Networks.

The manuscript studies a scheduling problem that arises when Federated LoRA deployments meet mobile edge links: the adaptation rank determines update size, transmission time, and the probability of timely delivery, yet existing systems fix it for all clients and rounds. We formulate the per-round decision as a joint client-rank assignment under communication-budget and participation constraints, solve it exactly with a pseudo-polynomial dynamic program, and stabilize heterogeneous-rank aggregation by freezing one LoRA factor at a shared initialization so that factor averaging coincides with update averaging.

The evaluation is two-tier. A real federated LoRA trainer (adapters on all convolutional and head layers of a BatchNorm CNN; non-IID CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/MNIST; five seeds) shows that rank choice preserves backbone accuracy (0.914 on CIFAR-10) while full-parameter updates spend 2.2x the bytes and lose most timely deliveries as deadlines tighten; matches fixed rank 16 on accuracy and delivery, matches fixed rank 16, and saves 14% of update bytes (five seeds). A 500-client scheduling simulator and a solver study then quantify the scheduling layer: adaptive rank is best or tied in all nine stress regimes, greedy heuristics lose up to 10% of expected useful updates in binding regimes, and the exact solver costs 33-836 ms per round at 500 clients.

All claims are reported with accuracy, timely-delivery ratio, and transmitted bytes separated; we explicitly claim no universal accuracy dominance. Code, configurations, and per-seed outputs will be made available in accordance with the journal's data and code policies. The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
