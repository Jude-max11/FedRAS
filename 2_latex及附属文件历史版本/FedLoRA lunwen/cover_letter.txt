Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "FedRAS: Network-Conditioned Rank Allocation for Federated LoRA" for publication in Computer Networks.

The manuscript studies a scheduling problem that arises when federated LoRA deployments meet mobile edge links: the adaptation rank determines update size, transmission time, and the probability of timely delivery, yet existing systems fix it for all clients and rounds, or adapt it from training state alone. We formulate the per-round decision as a joint client-rank assignment under communication-budget and participation constraints, score every client-rank pair with a payload-conditioned delivery hazard and a saturating capacity gain, solve the assignment exactly with a grid-based dynamic program, and stabilize heterogeneous-rank aggregation by freezing one LoRA factor at a shared initialization so that factor averaging coincides with update averaging.

The evaluation is two-tier. A real federated LoRA trainer (adapters on all convolutional and head layers of a BatchNorm CNN; non-IID CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/MNIST) shows that rank choice preserves backbone accuracy (0.914 on CIFAR-10) while full-parameter updates, at 58.7x the bytes of a rank-16 upload, miss every deadline under stress. Under a deadline chosen so that transmission genuinely binds, FedRAS delivers 22% more timely updates with 23% fewer bytes than fixed rank 16 at statistically equivalent accuracy (pre-registered TOST at a +/-0.5 percentage-point margin, ten seeds). A 500-client scheduler simulation and a solver study then quantify the scheduling layer: FedRAS is best or tied across nominal, stress, and binding-budget regimes, greedy heuristics lose 28-55% of expected useful updates when the budget binds (up to 64% for value-per-byte pricing), and the exact solver costs 23-421 ms per round at 500 clients, scaling linearly to 1000 clients.

An early poster version of this work appeared at MSWiM 2026, studying joint client-protection scheduling in space-air-ground mobile networks. The present submission is a substantially extended reformulation: the schedulable resource, the delivery and capacity models, the exact solver, the aggregation mechanism, the theoretical results, and the entire evaluation are new, and all numerical results are newly generated and not comparable with the poster version.

Accuracy, timely-delivery ratio, and transmitted bytes are reported as separate outcomes; we explicitly claim no universal accuracy dominance. Code, configurations, and per-seed outputs will be made available in accordance with the journal's data and code policies. The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
